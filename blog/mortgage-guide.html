Mortgage Guide for the USA

A mortgage is the biggest financial decision most Americans will ever make. Whether you're a first-time homebuyer or refinancing your current loan, understanding how mortgages work is critical. This guide explains monthly payments, amortization, PMI, taxes, escrow, loan types, and strategies for choosing the best deal.

This guide is based on U.S. mortgage rules and gives you everything you need to confidently navigate the market in 2025 and beyond.

What Is a Mortgage?

A mortgage is a long-term home loan where the property itself serves as collateral. Borrowers repay the loan monthly over 15 to 30 years, typically through a fixed payment that includes:

  • Principal - the amount borrowed
  • Interest - cost of borrowing
  • Property taxes
  • Homeowners insurance
  • PMI (Private Mortgage Insurance) if down payment < 20%

These components together form what lenders call a PITI payment.

How Mortgage Payments Work

Most U.S. mortgages use fixed monthly payments. Each month your payment is split between:

  • Interest - highest in early years
  • Principal - increases over time

This process is called amortization.

Your first payment is mostly interest. By the final years, most of your payment goes toward principal.

Interest Rate vs APR - What's the Difference?

Both terms appear on every mortgage quote, but they're not the same:

  • Interest Rate - the base cost of borrowing
  • APR (Annual Percentage Rate) - includes interest PLUS lender fees

APR gives a more accurate picture of your true loan cost.

Property Taxes & Homeowners Insurance

Most mortgage lenders require borrowers to pay:

  • Property tax — varies by county
  • Homeowners insurance — protects your home

These costs are often included in your monthly mortgage payment through an escrow account.

Understanding PMI (Private Mortgage Insurance)

If your down payment is less than 20%, you will likely pay PMI. PMI protects the lender - not the borrower - and increases your monthly payment.

PMI ends when your loan reaches:

  • 78% LTV (automatic removal)
  • 80% LTV (removal by request)

Types of Mortgages in the United States

1. Conventional Loan

Standard loans requiring good credit and steady income.

2. FHA Loan

Lower credit requirements, 3.5% down payment. Popular for first-time buyers.

3. VA Loan

No down payment and no PMI - exclusive to veterans and active military.

4. USDA Loan

Zero-down mortgages for rural and eligible suburban areas.

5. Jumbo Loan

Loans above conforming limits - used for high-value homes.

How Much Down Payment Should You Make?

Common down payment options include:

  • 3% - minimum for conventional loans
  • 3.5% - FHA minimum
  • 10% - stronger loan application
  • 20% - eliminates PMI

Mortgage Refinancing

Refinancing replaces your current mortgage with a new one - ideally with lower rates or a shorter term.

  • Rate-and-term refinance: lower interest or shorter term
  • Cash-out refinance: borrow against home equity

Mortgage Taxes & Deductions

Mortgage interest may be tax-deductible if you itemize deductions. Homeowners insurance and PMI typically are not deductible.

How to Choose the Best Mortgage

  • APR - not just interest rate
  • PMI rules
  • Down payment requirements
  • Lender fees
  • Loan term - 15-year vs 30-year
  • Escrow requirement
Always compare at least three lenders before choosing a mortgage.
